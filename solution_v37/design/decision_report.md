# Decision report - foresight-v37-ladder

## Overall approach

The run is a ladder: the ending evaluation rewards level most, and level is gained only at the half-year windows. The design therefore makes every decision serve the next window first and the ending second. Three ideas carry it.

1. Health first at the entry level. The run starts with Health in the middle of its scale, and the published failure directions name health reaching zero. In our logs the early deaths and most of the lost first windows came from story options that cost Health - overtime, carrying two jobs, evening commitments - taken for a promised gain. So until Health is healthy, no option or Energy action that costs Health is taken, and growth comes from the options that do the work at no Health cost; one point of Health recovery per quarter keeps the band.

2. Ceilings are watched, not assumed. The handbook says requirements rise with level and that thresholds are unpublished. In our runs each driver stopped responding at some value at each level and responded again after a promotion. The agent marks a driver at its ceiling only after two flat quarterly phases with an attempt in between, spends nothing more on it, and moves the points to the driver that still responds. No ceiling value is written in the Skills.

3. Risk is defused on a count, not on a feeling. Hidden risk is invisible and the HR remark lags. A defuse on a fixed schedule - after every missed window before the first promotion, at least every third quarterly phase after it, and at once when the remark says risks have piled up - kept risk control at its best in our longest runs. Story options that bury risk (silence, cover-ups, venting, leaks, shortcuts) are removed before any other comparison.

## Public information and in-run feedback used

Public: the 48-month structure, the quarterly Energy and main-action menus and their listed effects, the salary table, the grade meanings, the promotion factors (Skill, Network, Output), the failure directions and the ending dimensions. In-run: the visible statistics, the monthly feed (HR remark, review grade, promotion, warnings), the menu's status_updates and energy_cost, the event wording, and the agent's own notes with their expectations and lessons. The agent is told never to decide from a remembered value.

## Principal risks

- The first windows. Missing months 6 and 12 at the first level led to dismissal at month 24 in nearly every such run. The entry stage therefore aims every story choice and Energy point at reaching the ceilings before months 5 and 11, through options that cost no Health.
- Wording that steers the agent. In earlier versions, "guard" wording made the agent take self-protective options that cost Network and Output; "sprint" wording made it spend Health to zero. This version uses neither: it names the target and removes the costly kinds.
- Misread option effects. The agent often expects "no cost" from an option that costs a point. Every note states the expectation, and a miss produces a lesson that outranks the general reading rules.
- Late blow-ups. A second blow-up ends the run. The schedule above and the two post-blow-up quarters (no new risk, one defuse, lay low) are the answer.
- Token efficiency. Long internal analyses are resent at every call. The manifest limits reasoning to three short lines before each tool call.

## How the design generalises

Nothing in the Skills names an event, a title, a number of the simulation or a provider. The bands (7 and 4) are the team's own planning bands and are labelled as such; the ceilings are found inside the run; the defuse schedule counts quarterly phases, which the handbook defines. If event wording or order changes, the option reading still works from kinds of wording; if parameters change, the ledger and the expectation check adapt within the run; if the menu changes, quarter-plan reads the listed effects and prices from the menu itself rather than from memory.
